Checkout failed for 26 minutes because a merge shrank the connection pool from 20 to 5

A one-line config change shipped at 14:02 and sat harmless for 38 minutes — traffic was too low to notice. At 14:40 the nightly reconcile job opened its connections, drained the shrunken pool, and checkout error rate went from 0.1% to a peak of 34%. The first two responders spent 19 minutes on a payment provider that was never involved, because that provider happened to have an unrelated status-page notice open. A rollback at 15:03 fixed it; recovery completed at 15:07.

How
The prod override had been raised to 20 in PR #4102 (Feb). The branch was cut before that and the three-way merge resolved in favour of the stale side. The change was never intentional and is not mentioned in the PR description.

Why it happened, and why it took so long

Five contributing factors — the first caused it, the rest set the clock.
  1. A three-way merge reintroduced a stale value. +1/−1 inside a 340-line refactor. Review was genuine but the reviewer was reading the refactor, not the numbers.
  2. Nothing alerts on pool capacity. hikaricp_connections_max is scraped and graphed but has no rule; connections_pending has none either. The only alert that fired was the customer-facing symptom, 2m38s late.
  3. The 38-minute latency broke the causal link. By the time anyone looked, the deploy panel's default 30-minute window no longer showed it. “Nothing shipped recently” was technically what the tool said.
  4. An unrelated third-party notice acted as a decoy. The runbook's first step is “check third-party status”. It returned a plausible hit, and two responders anchored on it for 13 minutes. The disconfirming evidence — NordPay's flat auth success rate — was available the whole time.
  5. The batch job shares checkout's datasource. Even with pool size 20 this is a latent coupling; it is what turned a bad config into an outage at a specific minute rather than gradually.
