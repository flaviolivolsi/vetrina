Vetrina Gallery Ten pages, ten shapes

Ten pages that a paragraph could not have delivered.

Each one is a single self-contained HTML file, built by an agent that had not seen any of the others, given the finding the page had to carry and never the layout. They are here to show what the window is for: the page is shaped like the task, which is the one thing a dashboard shipped to everybody structurally cannot be.

Real projects, invented data. Gregario, Luxifare and Officina are mine. The incidents, numbers and findings in these pages are made up for the demo. The one page with an outage on it uses a fictional company on purpose.

How they were made

Ten agents, no shared context, none of them able to see another's page. Each was given the finding its page had to carry and never the layout, because a brief that describes markup has taken the agent's job and the result proves nothing.

They share one stylesheet, which ships with the daemon and is linked in a single line. It owns the frame, the spacing and the type, and deliberately owns nothing in the middle of the page: there is an axis, a track, a bar and a cell, and no component that draws a whole timeline. Whether that worked is checkable rather than arguable, so it was checked. The ten came out between 113 and 579 lines with eight distinct combinations of drawing primitives, and three of them use none at all.

Measured on every page at 320, 390 and 1440 pixels wide in both themes: no page scrolls sideways, none makes a network request, none logs an error, and every number stays readable with the stylesheet removed entirely.

The ten pages in evidence/ are a different thing and are deliberately untouched. Those were built with no stylesheet at all, to answer whether an unaided agent produces something worth looking at. Editing them to look nicer would stop them being evidence.
