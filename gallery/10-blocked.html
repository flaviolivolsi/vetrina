Three rules, pick one

A · B · C

All three are the same amount of work, roughly a day. They differ only in who ends up owning a ride when the answer isn't obvious.

AThe device wins

Every anonymous ride on the phone attaches to the new account, no prompt.

Nobody ever loses a ride. On a borrowed or shared phone you silently inherit a stranger's rides, and there is no way to hand them back.

If the email already has rides: keep both sets, dedupe by ride id, last write wins.

BThe session wins

Only rides from the anonymous session signed in at that moment attach.

Predictable and never wrong about ownership. Rides from an earlier anonymous session on the same phone stay local and are gone at the next reinstall — about 60 of the 412.

If the email already has rides: the server's rides stand, local extras attach around them.

CAsk the rider

Sign-up lists the local rides by date and distance; the rider ticks the ones that are theirs.

Correct in every case. Costs a new screen in the sign-up flow and asks a question at the moment people are least willing to answer one; expect drop-off.

If the email already has rides: the clash is shown and the rider resolves it.
