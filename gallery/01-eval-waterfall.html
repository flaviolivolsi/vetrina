King safety

Against · −74 cp

This is the term that decides the move, and it is the one the engine is least able to defend in words. Three sub-terms move at once: shelter quality, attacker count in the king zone, and light-square control around the castled king.

Fires because the g2 bishop carries the light squares around the king on its own, and the term weights that single point of failure heavily. Term weights shelter −29, attackers −27, diagonal −18.

▼

Pawn structure

Against · −38 cp

Opening the e-file cuts both ways. The d3 pawn ends up on a half-open file, and the two pawns defending it can only keep doing so by staying where they are.

Fires because c2 and e2 defend d3 only from their starting squares, so the term reads the queenside majority as frozen rather than mobile. Term weights backward pawn −24, half-open file exposure −14.

What the second system says out loud

5 of 16 evaluators fired

You are winning a clean pawn, and the e-file opens for whoever can use it first. That is the case for Nxe5, and it is worth about a pawn and a quarter on its own.

You are paying for it with the squares around your own king. The knight that leaves f6 was the piece holding that side together, and g3 does not heal. If you are going to play this, plan on spending a tempo on h3 or Re1 before Black's queen finds the diagonal.

The engine still plays it, at about three quarters of a pawn. Treat the score as a bet you have taken, not a position you have won.
